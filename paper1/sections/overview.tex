\section{Framework Overview and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Problem Setting}
\label{sec:framework:problem}

Let $G=(V,\mathcal{R},E)$ be a directed, labelled multigraph, where $V$ is the entity set, $\mathcal{R}$ is the relation vocabulary, and $E\subseteq V\times\mathcal{R}\times V$ is a multiset of triples. Each document-level graph is associated with source evidence $X$. A repair action $a$ is a typed graph transformation---completion, deletion, relation retyping, or a bounded bundle of these operations---and $T(G,a)$ denotes the resulting graph. Candidate actions may be proposed by deterministic rules, source-grounded extraction, or an LLM, but a proposal cannot modify $G$ directly.

This separation is central to our formulation. Prompted components generate candidates; numerical graph assessment determines the state; a routing policy decides whether repair is needed and which scope should receive priority; and explicit feasibility tests decide whether an edit can be committed. The method therefore treats an LLM as one intervention provider inside a constrained decision process, rather than as the decision process itself.

Defects are organized by the dependency horizon needed to detect them. The \emph{Entity-Scale} covers local incidence and duplicate facts, the \emph{Graph-Scale} covers schema and hierarchy violations, and the \emph{Context-Scale} checks whether triples are supported by the associated document. These scopes organize four operational quality dimensions used throughout the method and experiments; additional graph statistics can be incorporated as auxiliary features but are not part of the optimized profile reported here.

\subsection{Four-Dimensional Quality Profile}
\label{sec:framework:profile}

The assessment module reports percentage scores $Q_i(G)\in[0,100]$ and normalized scores $q_i(G)=Q_i(G)/100$. The operational profile is
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:quality_profile}
    \mathbf{q}(G)=
    \big[q_{\mathrm{conn}}(G),q_{\mathrm{uniq}}(G),q_{\mathrm{logic}}(G),q_{\mathrm{sem}}(G;X)\big]^\top
    \in[0,1]^4.
\end{equation}
The first two dimensions form the entity-scale sub-profile, logical consistency forms the graph-scale sub-profile, and semantic source support forms the context-scale sub-profile. The scalar diagnostic quality is
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:quality_scalar}
    \mathcal{Q}(G)=\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{q}(G),
    \qquad \mathbf{w}\in\Delta^3,
\end{equation}
where non-negative weights sum to one. This scalarization ranks trial states, while the individual dimensions remain visible to the constraint gate; a high aggregate score therefore cannot compensate for a prohibited per-dimension failure.

The profile provides the numerical interface between diagnosis and execution. It is augmented with graph statistics
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:decision_state}
    \mathbf{x}_t=[\mathbf{s}_t;\mathbf{g}_t],\qquad
    \mathbf{s}_t=100\,\mathbf{q}(G_t),\quad
    \mathbf{g}_t=[|V_t|,|E_t|,\rho(G_t),n_{\mathrm{viol}}(G_t)],
\end{equation}
which gives any learned or threshold-based router a fixed-dimensional view of the current graph without exposing it to prompt wording or raw generation traces.

\subsection{Constrained Sequential Decision Formulation}
\label{sec:framework:constraints}

A single edit can improve one quality dimension while damaging another. We therefore formulate repair as a finite-horizon constrained sequential decision problem. At step $t$, the system observes $(G_t,\mathbf{x}_t)$, constructs a finite candidate set $\mathcal{A}(G_t)$, selects $a_t$, and transitions according to
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:graph_transition}
    G_{t+1}=T(G_t,a_t).
\end{equation}
Let $\ell_i$ be the lower bound for profile dimension $i$ and $\delta_i$ its permitted one-step regression. To allow an initially deficient graph to recover instead of making every edit infeasible, define the restoration boundary
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:restoration_boundary}
    b_i(q)=
    \begin{cases}
        \max\{\ell_i,q-\delta_i\}, & q\geq\ell_i,\\
        q, & q<\ell_i.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
An action is feasible when
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:constrained_opt}
    \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{feas}}(G_t)=
    \left\{a\in\mathcal{A}(G_t):
    \begin{array}{l}
    q_i(T(G_t,a))\geq b_i(q_i(G_t)),\ \forall i,\\
    \rho(T(G_t,a))\leq\rho_{\max},\\
    T(G_t,a)\text{ satisfies the destructive-edit guards}
    \end{array}
    \right\}.
\end{equation}
The first condition preserves already feasible dimensions and requires a deficient dimension not to deteriorate. The density ceiling and destructive-edit guards prevent reward hacking through indiscriminate edge insertion or deletion.

For a horizon $T$, the ideal sequence maximizes discounted decision utility and terminal graph quality:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:sequence_objective}
    \max_{a_0,\ldots,a_{T-1}}
    \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^t U(a_t\mid G_t)
    +\gamma^T\mathcal{Q}(G_T),
    \quad
    \text{s.t. }a_t\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{feas}}(G_t),
\end{equation}
where $\gamma\in(0,1]$. Exhaustive sequence search is combinatorial. We use a receding-horizon policy: evaluate every current candidate on a trial graph, commit the highest-utility feasible action, recompute the profile and neural prior, and then re-plan. The selection is exact over the finite one-step candidate set; no claim of a globally optimal edit sequence is made.

\subsection{System Architecture}
\label{sec:framework:architecture}

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figure/method/image1.pdf}
    \caption{Framework architecture for a document-level KG. Scope-specific assessment maps the graph to a four-dimensional quality profile. A routing policy combines the profile with graph statistics to decide whether repair is required and to provide a soft scope prior. Rules, source-grounded modules, and LLM reasoning propose candidate edits; trial-state assessment and explicit constraints control every committed transition.}
    \label{fig:framework_overview}
\end{figure*}

The architecture contains three distinct layers. The assessment layer computes Eq.~(\ref{eq:quality_profile}) and detects violations. The policy layer maps Eq.~(\ref{eq:decision_state}) to a repair trigger and scope prior. The execution layer instantiates candidate transformations and exposes their trial states to the constraint gate. This division converts scope-specific diagnostic rules into measurable constraints, uses the profile to connect analysis and modification, and keeps the decision stage independent of the natural-language form used to propose an edit.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:abstract} gives the resulting control loop.
\begin{algorithm}[t]
    \caption{Profile-Conditioned Constrained KG Repair}
    \label{alg:abstract}
    \begin{algorithmic}[1]
        \Require KG $G_0$, source $X$, candidate providers $\mathcal{T}$, horizon $T$
        \Ensure Enhanced KG $G^*$
        \State $G^*\gets G_0$
        \For{$t=0$ \textbf{to} $T-1$}
        \State $(\mathbf{x}_t,\mathcal{V}_t)\gets\textsc{Assess}(G^*,X)$
        \State $(p_t,\boldsymbol{\pi}_t)\gets f_\phi(\mathbf{x}_t)$
        \If{$p_t<\tau_{\mathrm{repair}}$ and $\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{hard}}_t=\emptyset$} \textbf{break} \EndIf
        \State $\mathcal{A}_t\gets\textsc{Propose}(G^*,X,\mathcal{V}_t,\mathcal{T})$
        \State evaluate $T(G^*,a)$ and $U(a\mid G^*)$ for every $a\in\mathcal{A}_t$
        \State $\mathcal{F}_t\gets\{a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{feas}}(G^*):U(a\mid G^*)>0\}$
        \If{$\mathcal{F}_t=\emptyset$} \textbf{break} \EndIf
        \State $a_t^*\gets\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal{F}_t}U(a\mid G^*)$
        \State $G^*\gets T(G^*,a_t^*)$
        \EndFor
        \State \Return $G^*$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
