\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied document-graph repair through source-based generation and field
validation. The method combines structural normalization, optional diagnostic
context, one model call, and candidate filtering. On the Chinese record
benchmark, Diagnosis + Gate repairs 98.00\% of controlled defects and raises
F1 on extracted graphs from 87.99\% to 92.48\%. Direct field copying reaches
100\% on that source format.

Independent receipt text exposes field-selection and normalization errors.
With shared field definitions and consistent whitespace handling, adding an
evidence index improves F1 by 4.76 points on the frozen 60-document test
(95\% interval: 2.50 to 7.26).

Component replays show that action-cost penalties suppress controlled repairs,
while removing them does not improve natural-input F1. In the sampled model
responses, filtering acts mainly through source support and document-head checks.

Further same-document controls find equal mean F1 for the full index and
anchors alone, and do not establish a gain over random context. Field
definitions improve the full-index arm. The repair/extraction comparison
also shows why output-format and document-ID errors should be separated
from field selection.

On 56 held-out CUAD contracts, the indexed repair--re-extraction difference
is 0.72 points, with a 95\% interval from $-4.80$ to 6.48. Keeping the initial
graph therefore has no established advantage in this task. All seven failed
outputs remain in the analysis.

These results distinguish the full schema-aware repair step from the added
effects of evidence context and filtering. In the separate human audit,
134/199 determinate sampled edits pooled across five configurations are
acceptable, with one additional edit uncertain; the audit does not establish
whole-graph human F1. Remaining work includes relation-aware validation and
tests on a wider range of document schemas.
