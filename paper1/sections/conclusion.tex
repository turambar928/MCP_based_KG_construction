\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper addresses quality degradation in knowledge graphs constructed from text. We formulate enhancement as a profile-conditioned constrained sequential decision problem. Four bounded quality dimensions connect entity-, graph-, and context-scale diagnosis to a lightweight neural repair policy. Rules, source-grounded modules, and LLM reasoning propose typed transformations, while trial-state assessment, restoration-aware bounds, density control, and destructive-edit guards determine which transition can be committed. This separation makes prompted generation one candidate source inside a reproducible decision procedure.

The paired held-out benchmark provides direct evidence for repair effectiveness. Across 225 document graphs and 450 manifested defects, the full framework repairs 98.00\% of defects, preserves 99.32\% of clean facts, obtains 99.17\% triple F1, and exactly recovers 93.33\% of graphs. It exceeds direct LLM and ReAct-style repair under a shared model, with exact paired tests confirming that the differences are unlikely to arise from the discordant cases alone. The ablations localize the gains: contextual reasoning recovers missing and semantically corrupted values, structural preprocessing prevents avoidable model errors, and the final constraint gate reduces unsupported edits. The earlier aggregate $Q_{score}$ gain of 8.89 points remains consistent secondary evidence. In a controlled out-of-domain comparison, the extraction path behaves comparably to Neo4j LLM Knowledge Graph Builder.

The remaining errors define a narrower limitation than the earlier aggregate evaluation exposed. All nine unresolved test defects occur in long government legal or procedural fields, where exact span recovery fails for three invalid relations, two missing triples, one reversed edge, and three wrong values. Chunk-aware span selection is therefore a concrete next step. The benchmark uses balanced injected defects, exact matching, and one hosted base model, so future work should measure naturally occurring defect distributions, add expert adjudication for acceptable paraphrases, and repeat the comparison across model families. The router's perfect controlled-split result is a sanity check rather than a claim of natural-defect generalization.

%Future research will extend the framework along three directions. First, we will integrate domain-adaptive knowledge grounding mechanisms, incorporating curated expert lexicons and structured organizational registries to enhance semantic precision in specialized contexts. Second, the constraint optimization model will be extended to support temporal and causal logic, enabling the framework to validate dynamic workflows and time-sensitive dependencies. Third, we will investigate self-adaptive routing policies that learn activation thresholds from domain characteristics, reducing manual configuration overhead and improving out-of-distribution robustness. These extensions aim to transition the framework from a robust enhancement pipeline to an autonomous quality assurance system for production-scale knowledge graphs.
