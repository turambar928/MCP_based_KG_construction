\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied document-graph repair through source-based generation and field
validation. The pipeline normalizes the input, generates a candidate graph,
and checks heads, relations, source occurrence, and field counts. Matched
prompts and response-paired filtering separate generation choices from
candidate validation.

The results depend on source format. Direct field copying recovers every
reference in the serialized Chinese records. An evidence index improves F1
by 4.76 points on the frozen receipt test, but further controls do not establish
an advantage over random context or anchors alone. On contracts, indexed repair lowers F1 from 46.76\% to 44.84\%; its
0.72-point difference from re-extraction has a 95\% interval of
$[-4.80,6.48]$. The receipt benefit therefore does not establish transfer
to this contract task.
Diagnostic context and learned sequential selection also show no consistent
independent gain. Filtering removes some non-reference candidates through source-occurrence
and head checks, with small F1 changes and no factorial main effect
passing Holm correction.

Human review reveals a gap between reference agreement and semantic quality:
95/200 sampled discrepancies are actual input errors, and 134/199 determinate
system edits are acceptable, with one additional edit uncertain. The latter
sample pools five configurations. The low agreement on edit acceptability
also shows the difficulty of judging isolated edits. Future work should
validate the relation assigned to each source value and assess complete
repair outcomes across document schemas.
