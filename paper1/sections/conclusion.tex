\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper addresses the challenge of systematic quality degradation in knowledge graphs constructed from unstructured textual data. We propose a constraint-driven multi-scale enhancement framework that separates diagnostic evaluation from optimization. The architecture integrates a multi-scale quality assessment system (entity, graph, and context scales) with a hybrid completion engine governed by constraint-driven optimization and scale-aware routing. By checking relational dependencies across widening scopes, the framework pinpoints scale-specific deficits and applies targeted repair strategies within verifiable quality bounds.

Extensive experiments across government, finance, and environmental domains demonstrate that the framework recovers comprehensive quality scores by an average of 8.89 points, significantly outperforming rule-based (+7.14) and LLM-only (+4.46) baselines. Ablation studies confirm a synergistic gain of +2.30 points from multi-scale integration, validating the non-redundancy of scale-specific diagnosis. The constraint architecture effectively establishes quality lower bounds, while the routing mechanism improves optimization efficiency by dynamically aligning repair modalities with diagnosed deficit patterns. Statistical testing confirms the robustness and cross-domain generalization of the approach. The results indicate that separating scale-aware diagnosis from constrained optimization provides a principled way to recover quality systematically without compromising structural integrity.

Despite these advances, the framework has limitations in highly specialized settings. The semantic validation module relies on general-purpose LLMs, which are not well aligned with domain-specific regulatory vocabularies, leading to persistent terminology errors in specialized corpora. The current constraint set focuses on static structural and logical properties, without explicit modeling of temporal dependencies or dynamic procedural workflows. Additionally, the scale-aware routing mechanism depends on threshold-based trigger conditions that may require manual calibration when applied to novel domains with unfamiliar defect distributions.

%Future research will extend the framework along three directions. First, we will integrate domain-adaptive knowledge grounding mechanisms, incorporating curated expert lexicons and structured organizational registries to enhance semantic precision in specialized contexts. Second, the constraint optimization model will be extended to support temporal and causal logic, enabling the framework to validate dynamic workflows and time-sensitive dependencies. Third, we will investigate self-adaptive routing policies that learn activation thresholds from domain characteristics, reducing manual configuration overhead and improving out-of-distribution robustness. These extensions aim to transition the framework from a robust enhancement pipeline to an autonomous quality assurance system for production-scale knowledge graphs.
