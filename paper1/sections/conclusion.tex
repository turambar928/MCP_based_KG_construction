\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper studies constraint-guided repair of document-level knowledge graphs.
A four-dimensional profile connects local structure, graph constraints, and
source support to typed repair candidates. Deterministic rules and LLM modules
may propose edits, while trial-state checks decide which changes can be
committed.

The controlled benchmark shows that the Full System repairs 98.00\% of 450
defects and obtains 99.17\% triple F1. More importantly, it remains stronger
than an implementation-matched one-call Simple Pipeline: 98.00\% versus
95.11\% repair, with a paired $p=0.00235$. On 225 graphs produced by an actual
extraction call, 77 contain reference discrepancies. The Full System reduces
their error count by 27.75\% and reaches 92.48\% F1, while the Simple Pipeline
increases the error count and reaches 88.07\% F1. A proposal audit finds that
the constraint gate rejects eight unsupported non-reference triples, and the
Full System has the highest repair rate with both Claude Haiku and Gemma on a
fixed 60-case subset.

The added experiments also narrow the contribution. Learned routing matches
violation and threshold policies exactly under the evaluated features, so it
is an optional implementation rather than evidence for a uniquely effective
neural mechanism. Natural exact match remains 68.44\%, human adjudication of
acceptable reference alternatives is pending, and the evidence concerns small
document-level graphs rather than cross-document alignment or causal
reasoning. These boundaries motivate human-audited natural-error evaluation
and broader interconnected-graph repair as the next steps.

%Future research will extend the framework along three directions. First, we will integrate domain-adaptive knowledge grounding mechanisms, incorporating curated expert lexicons and structured organizational registries to enhance semantic precision in specialized contexts. Second, the constraint optimization model will be extended to support temporal and causal logic, enabling the framework to validate dynamic workflows and time-sensitive dependencies. Third, we will investigate self-adaptive routing policies that learn activation thresholds from domain characteristics, reducing manual configuration overhead and improving out-of-distribution robustness. These extensions aim to transition the framework from a robust enhancement pipeline to an autonomous quality assurance system for production-scale knowledge graphs.
