\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Knowledge Graphs (KGs) serve as a foundation for modern AI systems, providing structured representations that support complex reasoning, automated decision-making, and domain-specific applications. However, building high-quality KGs from unstructured text remains limited by noisy sources, differences across domains, and ambiguous extractions. The resulting graphs frequently exhibit structural fragmentation, logical inconsistencies, and semantic inaccuracies. As KGs move from curated benchmarks to large-scale production use, systematic quality enhancement has become essential for reliable and interpretable downstream applications.

Current quality enhancement methods mostly address one aspect of the problem at a time. Rule-based and ontology-driven validators enforce predefined constraints but suffer from fragile coverage and high maintenance cost. Embedding-based techniques improve scalability but lack explicit constraint satisfaction and interpretability. Recently, Large Language Models (LLMs) have been integrated for end-to-end extraction and semantic repair, demonstrating strong generalization capabilities. However, these approaches share three fundamental limitations. \textbf{First}, quality assessment and repair are typically decoupled, treating structural deduplication, logical validation, and semantic correction as independent post-processing steps without a unified objective. \textbf{Second}, defect diagnosis lacks scope awareness; uniform repair strategies are applied across heterogeneous error types, failing to distinguish between local topological anomalies, multi-hop logical violations, and cross-context functional dependencies. \textbf{Third}, without formal constraint guarantees the enhancement process is uncontrolled, often leading to metric-induced over-optimization (reward hacking), where gains in isolated scores accidentally break core logical properties or hurt overall graph quality.

To address these gaps, we formulate KG quality enhancement as a constraint-driven multi-scale optimization problem. We propose a framework comprising two integrated components: a multi-scale quality assessment system and a hybrid completion engine. The assessment system evaluates relational dependencies across three expanding scopes: the \textit{entity-scale} (explicit 1-hop topology), the \textit{graph-scale} (implicit multi-hop logical constraints), and the \textit{context-scale} (cross-document procedural and causal alignment). This yields a diagnostic profile that precisely localizes scale-specific deficits. Guided by this profile, the hybrid completion engine carries out targeted repair through two complementary mechanisms. First, a constraint-driven optimization strategy enforces explicit quality lower bounds on critical logical and structural properties while providing directional guidance for repair trajectories, effectively preventing uncontrolled degradation. Second, a scale-aware routing mechanism dynamically activates appropriate completion strategies (e.g., external retrieval, LLM reasoning, rule-based inference) according to the diagnosed deficit patterns, thereby improving optimization efficiency. By pairing scale-specific diagnosis with constrained optimization, the framework recovers quality systematically without compromising core reliability.

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Multi-Scale Quality Assessment System:} We introduce a diagnostic framework that progressively validates relational dependencies across entity, graph, and context scales, generating a comprehensive profile that localizes scale-specific deficits without cross-scale interference.
	\item \textbf{Constraint-Driven Optimization with Scale-Aware Routing:} We formulate KG enhancement as a constrained optimization problem that enforces verifiable quality lower bounds and provides directional repair guidance. Integrated with a routing mechanism that dynamically activates targeted completion strategies, this design improves optimization efficiency and mitigates metric-induced over-optimization.
	\item \textbf{Comprehensive Empirical Validation:} Through systematic degradation experiments across government, finance, and environmental domains, we demonstrate that the framework recovers comprehensive quality scores by an average of 8.89 points, outperforming rule-based and LLM-only baselines. Ablation studies confirm a +2.30 synergistic gain from multi-scale integration, validating the architectural non-redundancy and cross-domain generalization.
\end{itemize}
