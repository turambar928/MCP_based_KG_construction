\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Knowledge graphs (KGs) serve as foundational infrastructure for cognitive AI, enabling structured semantic representations that support complex reasoning and automated decision-making. By transforming unstructured information into explicit relational triples, KGs provide a solid foundation for downstream applications such as question answering, recommendation systems, and domain-specific analytics \citep{ref_ji2021,ref_dong2014}. Their capacity to encode both explicit facts and implicit semantic associations makes them indispensable in modern data-driven ecosystems. As AI systems increasingly rely on structured knowledge for high-stakes decision-making, the reliability and trustworthiness of the underlying KGs have become paramount.

Despite their critical role, constructing high-quality KGs from unstructured natural language inherently introduces structural fragmentation and factual inaccuracies, necessitating post-processing enhancement to ensure data reliability \citep{ref_zaveri2016, ref_paulheim2017}. Extraction pipelines are fundamentally constrained by source noise, domain heterogeneity, and extraction ambiguities, inevitably producing defects such as isolated nodes, logical conflicts, and semantic misalignments \citep{ref_ji2021,ref_wang2021}. As KGs transition from curated benchmarks to production-scale deployments, these inherent flaws severely undermine the robustness of downstream tasks. Therefore, systematic quality enhancement is not merely a technical fix for data flaws, but a critical prerequisite for ensuring the usability and generalization capability of KGs in real-world scenarios.

To mitigate these quality defects, existing KG construction pipelines integrate rule-based validators \citep{ref_shacl2017,ref_kontokostas2014}, embedding-based scoring \citep{ref_bordes2013,ref_nickel2016}, and large language model (LLM) driven refinement. Statistical anomaly detection and constraint-based validators identify numerical outliers and explicit constraint violations \citep{ref_kontokostas2014,ref_wienand2014}. Embedding-based models estimate triple plausibility, but a plausibility score alone does not establish compliance with application-specific constraints \citep{ref_bordes2013,ref_nickel2016}. Lin et al. evaluate LLM-based repair of SHACL violations and find that prompts containing relevant constraints and graph context improve repair performance \citep{ref_lin2025}. These complementary capabilities motivate combining structural diagnostics, source evidence, and explicit validation.

Quality assessment can draw on local constraint checks, graph-level plausibility estimates, and constraint-aware LLM repair evaluation \citep{ref_kontokostas2014,ref_nickel2016,ref_lin2025}. These methods inspect different kinds of evidence. Local topological correctness does not establish global logical consistency, and a plausible triple is not necessarily supported by its source. Our design therefore combines scale-specific signals into a shared diagnostic profile.

To address this limitation, we propose a \emph{Multi-Scale Quality Assessment} framework that continuously expands relational dependency scopes across three levels: Entity-Scale (1-hop explicit topology validation), Graph-Scale (multi-hop implicit logical constraint verification), and Context-Scale (cross-document causal and process chain validation). This framework generates a unified diagnostic profile that quantifies scale-specific deficits, enabling precise defect localization aligned with structural contexts and providing directional guidance for subsequent optimization.

A further design requirement is to control which proposed changes are accepted. Completion models such as TransE, DistMult, and ConvE learn scores for missing-link prediction \citep{ref_bordes2013,ref_yang2015,ref_dettmers2018}; their scoring objectives do not by themselves constitute a post-edit validator for an application's quality bounds. Prior work already incorporates logical rules and type constraints into embedding learning \citep{ref_guo2016,ref_ding2018}, and constraint-aware LLM repair has also been studied \citep{ref_lin2025}. Meanwhile, hallucination remains a documented risk of natural language generation \citep{ref_ji2023}. These findings motivate explicit source-support and feasibility checks when generative outputs are used to edit a KG. As refinement surveys emphasize, completion and error correction address different aspects of graph quality \citep{ref_paulheim2017}; a repair procedure must account for both.

To establish verifiable optimization boundaries, we introduce \emph{Constraint-Driven Optimization} that defines feasible repair regions across multiple scales. Positive constraints set quality lower bounds at each scale (structural integrity at Entity-Scale, logical consistency at Graph-Scale, semantic coherence at Context-Scale), providing directional gradients for repair. Negative constraints impose a density upper bound, a per-dimension regression guard, and a least-destructive cost function to penalize unnecessary modifications. This multi-scale constraint duality suppresses metric-induced over-completion and uncontrolled pruning, ensuring that optimization improves targeted quality dimensions without degrading the overall graph reliability.

Heterogeneous repair tools also require coordination. Chain-of-thought prompting elicits intermediate reasoning \citep{ref_wei2023}, retrieval-augmented generation conditions outputs on retrieved evidence \citep{ref_lewis2020}, and ReAct interleaves reasoning with actions \citep{ref_yao2023react}. These studies establish useful reasoning and interaction mechanisms, but do not specify a defect-conditioned policy for our KG repair setting. We therefore investigate routing based on the diagnosed graph state, so that the repair procedure can allocate tools according to the evidence and structural scope of a violation.

To resolve this, we design a \emph{Scale-Aware Routing} mechanism that dynamically maps diagnosed deficits to enhancement strategies. A lightweight neural network integrates the multi-scale quality assessment profile and constraint-checking violation set to compute a strategy activation matrix. This targeted routing assigns rule-based inference to structural violations, source-grounded completion to factual gaps, and LLM reasoning to semantic inconsistencies. By aligning computational effort with diagnosed defect complexity, the router provides a practical gate for reducing projected repair cost while isolating cross-scale interference.

Building on these methodological advancements, we integrate them into a unified constraint-driven multi-scale optimization framework. In this closed-loop architecture, the multi-scale assessment system serves as the diagnostic foundation to identify scale-specific deficits and generate precise diagnostic profiles. These profiles simultaneously feed into the constraint-driven optimizer to define feasible repair regions, and the scale-aware router acting as an intelligent dispatcher to allocate optimal strategies. Together, they form a tightly coupled hybrid completion engine where diagnosis dictates boundaries, boundaries constrain routing, and routing executes minimal-destructive repairs. This integration ensures that every intervention is precisely diagnosed, strictly bounded, and efficiently executed, achieving systematic quality recovery without compromising graph integrity or inflating computational costs.

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Multi-Scale Quality Assessment Framework:} We establish a comprehensive evaluation system that progressively validates relational dependencies across entity, graph, and context scales. Based on this system, we generate unified diagnostic profiles that precisely localize scale-specific deficits and provide directional guidance for subsequent repair processes, fundamentally resolving cross-scale misdiagnosis.
	\item \textbf{Constraint-Driven Quality Improvement Method:} We propose a constrained optimization approach that enforces verifiable quality boundaries through positive and negative constraint duality to prevent metric-induced over-optimization. Integrated with a lightweight neural routing mechanism, this method dynamically activates source-grounded completion, LLM reasoning, and rule-based inference to resolve heterogeneous defects while minimizing intervention destructiveness.
	\item \textbf{Paired and Auditable Empirical Validation:} We construct a document-grouped benchmark with 1,499 clean/corrupted graph pairs and 2,998 manifested defects. On 225 held-out documents, the framework repairs 98.00\% of 450 defects with 99.32\% clean-fact preservation and significantly outperforms direct LLM and ReAct-style repair. We archive case-level outputs, bootstrap intervals, paired tests, component ablations, an exhaustive failure audit, and an independent semantic reliability check.
\end{itemize}
