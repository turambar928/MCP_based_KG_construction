\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Knowledge graphs (KGs) serve as foundational infrastructure for cognitive AI, enabling structured semantic representations that support complex reasoning and automated decision-making. By transforming unstructured information into explicit relational triples, KGs provide a solid foundation for downstream applications such as question answering \cite{ref_zhu2023}, recommendation systems \cite{ref_guo2020}, and domain-specific analytics \cite{ref_dong2014}. Their capacity to encode both explicit facts and implicit semantic associations makes them indispensable in modern data-driven ecosystems. As AI systems increasingly rely on structured knowledge for high-stakes decision-making, the reliability and trustworthiness of the underlying KGs have become paramount.

Despite their critical role, constructing high-quality KGs from unstructured natural language inherently introduces structural fragmentation and factual inaccuracies, necessitating post-processing enhancement to ensure data reliability \cite{ref_zaveri2016, ref_paulheim2017}. Extraction pipelines are fundamentally constrained by source noise, domain heterogeneity, and extraction ambiguities, inevitably producing defects such as isolated nodes, logical conflicts, and semantic misalignments \cite{ref_zeng2021}. As KGs transition from curated benchmarks to production-scale deployments, these inherent flaws severely undermine the robustness of downstream tasks. Therefore, systematic quality enhancement is not merely a technical fix for data flaws, but a critical prerequisite for ensuring the usability and generalization capability of KGs in real-world scenarios.

To mitigate these inherent quality defects, existing KG construction pipelines have progressively integrated various enhancement techniques, ranging from rule-based validators \cite{ref_bansal2023} to embedding-based scoring \cite{ref_sun2019} and large language model (LLM) driven refinement. Statistical anomaly detection and constraint-based validators effectively flag local structural violations but lack the semantic capacity to resolve complex factual errors \cite{ref_kontokostas2014, ref_wienand2014}. Embedding-based models evaluate plausibility through continuous vector spaces yet cannot verify explicit logical consistency \cite{ref_bordes2013, ref_nickel2016}. Conversely, LLM-driven approaches capture global semantic coherence but overlook fine-grained multi-hop logical dependencies \cite{ref_lin2025}. Despite these advancements, when facing complex quality degradation in real-world scenarios, these isolated technical trajectories still expose fundamental limitations.

Constructing Knowledge Graphs (KGs) from unstructured natural language inherently introduces structural fragmentation and factual inaccuracies, necessitating post-processing enhancement to ensure data reliability \cite{ref_dong2014, ref_zaveri2016, ref_paulheim2017}. However, current quality assessment methods operate within isolated granularity boundaries. Statistical anomaly detection and constraint-based validators effectively flag local structural violations but lack the semantic capacity to resolve complex factual errors \cite{ref_kontokostas2014, ref_paulheim2014, ref_wienand2014}. Embedding-based models evaluate plausibility through continuous vector spaces yet cannot verify explicit logical consistency \cite{ref_bordes2013, ref_nickel2016}. LLM-driven approaches capture global semantic coherence but overlook fine-grained multi-hop logical dependencies \cite{ref_lin2025}. This single-granularity evaluation paradigm induces cross-scale misdiagnosis: local topological correctness does not guarantee global logical consistency, and global semantic plausibility cannot ensure local structural fidelity.

To address this limitation, we propose a \emph{Multi-Scale Quality Assessment} framework that continuously expands relational dependency scopes across three levels: Entity-Scale (1-hop explicit topology validation), Graph-Scale (multi-hop implicit logical constraint verification), and Context-Scale (cross-document causal and process chain validation). This framework generates a unified diagnostic profile that quantifies scale-specific deficits, enabling precise defect localization aligned with structural contexts and providing directional guidance for subsequent optimization.

Beyond assessment limitations, existing KG enhancement methods critically lack explicit boundary constraints during optimization, leading to uncontrolled and potentially destructive graph modifications. Embedding-based completion optimizes for plausibility scores without verifying domain/range compatibility or cardinality constraints, frequently generating triples that violate established ontological rules \cite{ref_bordes2013, ref_yang2015, ref_dettmers2018}. LLM-driven semantic repair pursues fluency but introduces hallucinated relations unsupported by source evidence \cite{ref_ji2023, ref_lin2025}. More fundamentally, unconstrained optimization creates irreconcilable conflicts among quality dimensions: completeness-driven link prediction injects relations that violate consistency constraints, while accuracy-oriented error correction triggers aggressive pruning that destroys valid but low-frequency structural dependencies \cite{ref_paulheim2017, ref_nickel2016}. These uncontrolled modifications render the repaired KG locally improved yet globally unreliable, severely degrading its foundational integrity and downstream generalization capability.

To establish verifiable optimization boundaries, we introduce \emph{Constraint-Driven Optimization} that defines feasible repair regions across multiple scales. Positive constraints set quality lower bounds at each scale (structural integrity at Entity-Scale, logical consistency at Graph-Scale, semantic coherence at Context-Scale), providing directional gradients for repair. Negative constraints impose density upper bounds, task-relevance thresholds, and a least-destructive cost function to penalize unnecessary modifications. This multi-scale constraint duality suppresses metric-induced over-completion and uncontrolled pruning, ensuring that optimization improves targeted quality dimensions without degrading the overall graph reliability.

Furthermore, current KG enhancement pipelines lack systematic guidance for orchestrating heterogeneous repair tools. Existing frameworks typically apply uniform strategies, indiscriminately stacking rule-based inference, external retrieval, and LLM reasoning without adaptive coordination \cite{ref_wei2023, ref_lewis2020}. This blind activation fails to match strategy complexity with defect severity: deploying computationally expensive LLM reasoning for simple structural violations wastes resources, while relying on rigid rules for deep semantic defects yields suboptimal repairs \cite{ref_pan2023, ref_ji2023}. The absence of a principled routing mechanism also introduces cross-scale interference, where concurrent heterogeneous strategies produce conflicting modifications that degrade overall optimization efficiency.

To resolve this, we design a \emph{Scale-Aware Routing} mechanism that dynamically maps diagnosed deficits to optimal enhancement strategies. A lightweight neural network integrates the multi-scale quality assessment profile and constraint-checking violation set to compute a strategy activation matrix. This targeted routing ensures that each detected deficit triggers the most efficient completion modality—rule-based inference for structural violations, retrieval-augmented generation for factual gaps, and LLM reasoning for semantic inconsistencies. By aligning computational effort with actual defect complexity, our approach significantly reduces the time and economic overhead of KG quality enhancement while effectively isolating cross-scale interference.

Building on these methodological advancements, we integrate them into a unified constraint-driven multi-scale optimization framework. In this closed-loop architecture, the multi-scale assessment system serves as the diagnostic foundation to identify scale-specific deficits and generate precise diagnostic profiles. These profiles simultaneously feed into the constraint-driven optimizer to define feasible repair regions, and the scale-aware router acting as an intelligent dispatcher to allocate optimal strategies. Together, they form a tightly coupled hybrid completion engine where diagnosis dictates boundaries, boundaries constrain routing, and routing executes minimal-destructive repairs. This integration ensures that every intervention is precisely diagnosed, strictly bounded, and efficiently executed, achieving systematic quality recovery without compromising graph integrity or inflating computational costs.

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Multi-Scale Quality Assessment Framework:} We establish a comprehensive evaluation system that progressively validates relational dependencies across entity, graph, and context scales. Based on this system, we generate unified diagnostic profiles that precisely localize scale-specific deficits and provide directional guidance for subsequent repair processes, fundamentally resolving cross-scale misdiagnosis.
	\item \textbf{Constraint-Driven Quality Improvement Method:} We propose a constrained optimization approach that enforces verifiable quality boundaries through positive and negative constraint duality to prevent metric-induced over-optimization. Integrated with a lightweight neural routing mechanism, this method dynamically activates targeted completion strategies (external retrieval, LLM reasoning, rule-based inference) to efficiently resolve heterogeneous defects while minimizing intervention destructiveness.
	\item \textbf{Comprehensive Empirical Validation:} Through systematic degradation experiments across government, finance, and environmental domains, we demonstrate that the framework recovers comprehensive quality scores by an average of 8.89 points, significantly outperforming rule-based and LLM-only baselines. Ablation studies confirm a +2.30 synergistic gain from multi-scale integration, validating the architectural non-redundancy and cross-domain generalization.
\end{itemize}
