\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Knowledge Graph Construction and Quality-Aware Extraction}

Traditional Knowledge Graph (KG) construction follows a systematic pipeline comprising information extraction, entity linking, relation mapping, and graph assembly \cite{ref_ji2021}. Early systems such as DBpedia \cite{ref_auer2007}, YAGO \cite{ref_suchanek2007}, and Freebase \cite{ref_bollacker2008} extract facts from structured and semi-structured sources. During the construction process, quality-aware mechanisms are actively employed to ensure data reliability. These mechanisms include confidence-based filtering, source voting, and entity disambiguation \cite{ref_dong2014}. Probabilistic knowledge fusion techniques integrate multiple extraction signals to assign reliability scores to generated triples, establishing a foundational layer of quality control directly within the extraction pipeline \cite{ref_dong2014}.

With the advancement of representation learning, embedding-based models map entities and relations into continuous vector spaces to support scalable graph assembly and downstream inference \cite{ref_bordes2013, ref_wang2017}. Graph Neural Network (GNN) based architectures aggregate neighborhood information to capture complex topological dependencies during the construction phase \cite{ref_schlichtkrull2018}. Recently, Large Language Models (LLMs) facilitate end-to-end Text-to-KG pipelines \cite{ref_zhang2023}. These approaches utilize prompting techniques \cite{ref_wei2023}, fine-tuning \cite{ref_pan2025}, and retrieval-augmented generation \cite{ref_lewis2020} to perform extraction, semantic alignment, and factual verification \cite{ref_pan2023, ref_white2023}. The integration of these neural and generative models provides diverse strategies for building and maintaining the semantic integrity of KGs from unstructured text.

\subsection{Knowledge Graph Quality Assessment}

Knowledge graph quality assessment characterizes graph reliability across multiple dimensions, including accuracy, completeness, consistency, and timeliness \cite{ref_zaveri2016, ref_paulheim2017}. Automated assessment methods evaluate these dimensions using diverse computational techniques. Statistical anomaly detection models value distributions to identify irregularities in numerical and literal data \cite{ref_wienand2014, ref_paulheim2014}. Embedding-based models evaluate triple plausibility by calculating distances in continuous vector spaces, providing a global semantic perspective on graph integrity \cite{ref_nickel2016}. Rule-based validation frameworks verify logical structures and schema compliance using formal constraint languages, ensuring explicit ontological consistency \cite{ref_kontokostas2014}. These methodologies provide comprehensive metrics for evaluating graph quality from local structural to global semantic perspectives.

Recent studies explore LLM-based semantic evaluation for fine-grained quality assessment, leveraging pre-trained linguistic knowledge to validate complex relational facts \cite{ref_lin2025}. Crowd-sourcing paradigms complement automated methods by leveraging human intelligence for ambiguous quality judgments \cite{ref_acosta2013}. Furthermore, assessment frameworks are designed to operate across varying structural scopes, ranging from single-triple validation to multi-hop path consistency and cross-document context verification. By generating diagnostic profiles across these different granularities, existing assessment systems provide directional guidance for identifying specific types of structural and semantic deviations within large-scale knowledge bases \cite{ref_xue2023}.

\subsection{Knowledge Graph Quality Enhancement and Tool Orchestration}

KG enhancement techniques aim to detect and correct structural and factual anomalies to improve overall graph reliability. Completion methods based on relational paths \cite{ref_lao2011}, embeddings \cite{ref_bordes2013, ref_yang2015, ref_shi2017}, and neural architectures \cite{ref_dettmers2018} predict missing links to improve graph completeness. Reinforcement learning approaches reason over paths to discover indirect evidence for link prediction and error correction \cite{ref_das2018}. Meta-learning frameworks address few-shot link prediction scenarios \cite{ref_chen2019}. Rule-mining systems discover logical patterns to guide consistency checking, while neuro-symbolic methods integrate logical rules or type information into embedding learning objectives to align structural and semantic representations during the repair process.

Building upon traditional rule-mining systems, recent advancements have introduced neural and generative method to automatically discover logical rules and utilize them for KG quality enhancement. Generative rule mining approaches, such as RNNLogic \cite{ref_qu2021}, employ generative models to automatically discover logical rules and optimize them jointly with knowledge representations, thereby guiding link prediction and structural completion. Neural-symbolic architectures, exemplified by NBFNet \cite{ref_zhu2021}, integrate neural networks with symbolic path reasoning to implicitly mine and apply relational rules for error correction and inference. Furthermore, probabilistic logic frameworks like pLogicNet \cite{ref_qu2019} combine first-order logic rules with neural embeddings, utilizing mined rules as structural priors to constrain the generation of new facts and the correction of noisy triples. More recently, the integration of Large Language Models has enabled the automatic extraction of explicit logical rules and negative constraints directly from unstructured corpora or existing graphs \cite{ref_pan2023, ref_zhang2023}. These LLM-extracted rules serve as verifiable semantic boundaries to refine KG quality. By incorporating these dynamically mined rules as formal constraints, our framework leverages them to bound the multi-scale repair loop, ensuring that the enhancement process remains logically consistent and structurally sound.

LLM-driven reasoning is applied for semantic repair and knowledge fusion, utilizing generative capabilities to resolve deep semantic inconsistencies \cite{ref_lin2025, ref_lewis2020, ref_wei2023}. To manage the diverse array of available repair tools, recent research explores adaptive tool orchestration and routing mechanisms. These systems integrate heterogeneous strategies, such as rule-based inference, external retrieval, and LLM reasoning, into unified pipelines. By computing activation matrices or routing scores based on the specific characteristics of detected defects, these orchestration frameworks dynamically allocate computational resources to the most appropriate enhancement modality. These methodologies provide diverse strategies for restoring graph quality across different structural scopes, forming the foundation for advanced, resource-efficient quality optimization frameworks.
