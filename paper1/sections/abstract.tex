\begin{abstract}

Constructing high-quality knowledge graphs (KGs) from unstructured text is inherently constrained by source noise and extraction ambiguities, yielding structural fragmentation, logical inconsistencies, and semantic inaccuracies. Existing enhancement methods typically suffer from decoupled assessment and enhancement, lack explicit boundary constraints leading to metric-induced over-optimization, and apply uniform strategies that fail to match defect complexity. To address these limitations, we propose a constraint-driven multi-scale optimization framework that integrates diagnostic evaluation and targeted enhancement into a closed-loop system. Specifically, a multi-scale quality assessment system progressively validates relational dependencies across entity, graph, and context scales to generate precise diagnostic profiles. Guided by these profiles, a constraint-driven optimization mechanism enforces verifiable quality boundaries through positive and negative constraints to prevent destructive modifications. Concurrently, a scale-aware routing mechanism dynamically dispatches heterogeneous enhancement tools (external retrieval, rule-based inference, and LLM reasoning) aligned with specific deficit patterns. Systematic experiments across government, finance, and environmental domains demonstrate that the framework recovers comprehensive quality scores by an average of 8.89 points, significantly outperforming rule-based and LLM-only baselines. Ablation studies confirm a synergistic gain of 2.30 points from multi-scale integration, validating the framework's cross-domain generalization and architectural non-redundancy.
	
\keywords{Knowledge Graph, Quality Assessment, Constraint-Driven Optimization, Large Language Models}

\end{abstract}
