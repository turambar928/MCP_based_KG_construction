The construction of knowledge graphs (KGs) from unstructured text frequently yields structural fragmentation, logical inconsistencies, and semantic inaccuracies. Existing enhancement methods typically address isolated metrics or apply the same repair strategy everywhere, lacking a unified diagnostic-repair loop. This separation often leads to uncontrolled graph modifications and metric-induced over-optimization. 
To address these limitations, we propose a constraint-driven multi-scale framework for KG quality enhancement, consisting of a multi-scale quality assessment system and a hybrid completion engine. The assessment system evaluates relational dependencies across entity, graph, and context scales, generating a diagnostic profile across multiple scales. Guided by this profile, the hybrid completion engine employs constraint-driven optimization to enforce explicit quality lower bounds and provide directional guidance for quality enhancement. Integrated with a scale-aware routing mechanism, this design dynamically activates targeted completion strategies (e.g., external retrieval, LLM reasoning, rule-based inference), thereby improving optimization efficiency.
Systematical experiments on real-world datasets across government, finance, and environmental domains demonstrate that our framework recovers comprehensive quality scores by an average of 8.89 points, outperforming rule-based (+7.14) and LLM-only (+4.46) baselines. Ablation studies confirm a synergistic gain of +2.30 points from multi-scale integration. The framework exhibits adaptive constraint prioritization and cross-domain generalization, providing a structured approach for reliable KG quality enhancement.
