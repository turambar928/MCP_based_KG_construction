\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Building Knowledge Graphs}

Building a KG involves extracting facts, linking entities, mapping relations,
and joining the results \citep{ref_ji2021}. DBpedia \citep{ref_auer2007},
YAGO \citep{ref_suchanek2007}, and Freebase \citep{ref_bollacker2008} build
knowledge bases from data with clear or partly clear structure.
Knowledge fusion uses source evidence to score facts \citep{ref_dong2014}.
Our study repairs graphs after extraction.

Embedding models use vectors to predict links
\citep{ref_bordes2013,ref_wang2017}. Graph neural networks learn from nearby
nodes and relations \citep{ref_schlichtkrull2018}. Reviews describe the use
of LLMs to build, complete, and combine KGs
\citep{ref_pan2023,ref_pan2025,ref_bian2025}. Prompt methods include
chain-of-thought reasoning \citep{ref_wei2023} and shared prompt patterns
\citep{ref_white2023}. Retrieval-augmented generation adds source evidence
to model inputs \citep{ref_lewis2020}. Neo4j Labs' LLM Knowledge Graph
Builder turns documents into graphs with user-set schemas
\citep{ref_neo4j_llm_graph_builder}. We compare with its core extractor in
Section~\ref{sec:eval:external_benchmark}.

\subsection{Checking Graph Quality}

KG quality covers correct facts, missing facts, rule agreement, and current
information \citep{ref_zaveri2016,ref_paulheim2017}. Statistical methods
find unusual values \citep{ref_wienand2014,ref_paulheim2014}.
Embedding models score how likely a triple is \citep{ref_nickel2016}.
Rule languages check the graph's schema \citep{ref_kontokostas2014}.
Each method checks a different part of graph quality.

Lin et al.\ add rule errors to graphs and compare LLM repair prompts
\citep{ref_lin2025}. They use SHACL rules and nearby graph context.
Human review helps check cases with unclear answers \citep{ref_acosta2013}.
Other reviews cover quality checks during graph building and repair
\citep{ref_wang2021,ref_paulheim2017}. Our error report lists duplicate
triples, schema errors, and values that are missing from the source text.

\subsection{Repairing Graphs and Choosing Actions}

KG completion predicts missing links. Methods use paths
\citep{ref_lao2011}, embeddings
\citep{ref_bordes2013,ref_yang2015,ref_shi2017}, and neural networks
\citep{ref_dettmers2018}. MINERVA learns to follow graph paths to answer
queries \citep{ref_das2018}. MetaR predicts links from a few examples
\citep{ref_chen2019}. AMIE learns positive rules \citep{ref_galarraga2013}.
RuDiK learns positive and negative rules \citep{ref_ortona2018}.
Other methods add rules to embedding training
\citep{ref_guo2016,ref_ding2018}.

RNNLogic trains a rule generator and an answer predictor together
\citep{ref_qu2021}. NBFNet learns from graph paths to predict links
\citep{ref_zhu2021}. pLogicNet joins logical rules and embeddings
\citep{ref_qu2019}. These methods combine rules with learned predictions.
We combine source text with field checks and compare filtering with a
selector that scores trial edits.

Lin et al.\ compare SHACL-based repair prompts \citep{ref_lin2025}.
ReAct joins model reasoning with actions that gather information
\citep{ref_yao2023react}. We use this pattern in the ReAct-style baseline.
Our SHACL-context baseline adapts Lin et al.'s five prompt parts to the same
field task and call limit. Shared prompts and scores before and after
filtering show the effects of context and graph checks.
