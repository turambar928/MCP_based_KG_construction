\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

We presented a graph--rule co-optimization framework with a trained Double-DQN scheduler and dual-strategy text-based rule generation. The scheduling environment exposes the dependence between validator acquisition and graph repair. Its implementation includes replay, target-network updates, feasibility masks, and archived graph transitions.

With introduced violations measured after each edit, Double DQN produces no new violations on the ten evaluation scenarios, but reaches lower structural quality than the acquire-then-deficit heuristic. Its higher mean discounted return reflects the reward's penalty for newly introduced violations. Retrained ablations support the action mask's final-quality contribution; independent gains from the other tested components are not established after correction.

Deletion and augmentation contribute different candidates on the same documents. At equal call budgets, augmentation produces more constraint candidates. An offline bridge now connects exact archived patterns to detection, action availability and record removal on a government-typed suite. The next step is to test learned scheduling with those generated-rule interfaces under varied rule arrivals, then evaluate factual repairs on natural defects and held-out domains. This would establish when text-derived acquisition benefits adaptive scheduling beyond the fixed registry.
