\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

We presented a graph--rule co-optimization framework with a trained Double-DQN scheduler and dual-strategy text-based rule generation. The scheduling environment captures the dependence between validator acquisition and graph repair, with replay, target-network updates, feasibility masks and archived graph transitions.

The reward study explains why a policy can introduce no violations yet leave useful repairs undone. Counting temporary duplicates makes relation restoration unattractive under the original reward. An initial-size rate penalty raises Double-DQN reference-triple F1 from 94.67\% to 99.49\% on reserved corruption scenarios. Zero penalty and simple controls also perform strongly, and the acquire-then-deficit heuristic reaches 99.91\%. The evidence supports correcting the penalty while leaving the benefit of learned scheduling over these controls unresolved.

Current-rate ablations support the role of the action mask: removing it
lowers F1 by 2.50 points after multiplicity correction. A development-calibrated
small count penalty remains statistically indistinguishable from the rate
penalty at the base graph size.

Deletion and augmentation contribute different candidates on the same documents; augmentation produces more constraints at equal call budgets. An offline bridge connects exact archived patterns to detection, action availability and record removal on a government-typed suite. A typed-document development pilot compiles both strategies but triggers no repairs. Completing the learned integration requires generated rules with verified repair effects, followed by comparison against the same simple controls. Natural-error labels and held-out document domains would then test factual correctness and transfer beyond controlled corruption.
