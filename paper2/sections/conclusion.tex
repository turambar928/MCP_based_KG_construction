\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

We presented a graph--rule co-optimization framework with a trained Double-DQN scheduler and dual-strategy text-based rule generation. The scheduling environment exposes the dependence between validator acquisition and graph repair. Its implementation includes replay, target-network updates, feasibility masks, and archived graph transitions.

The matched offline study clarifies which parts help in the controlled setting. Double DQN improves on feasible fixed schedules but is slightly outperformed by an acquire-then-deficit heuristic at equal acquisition cost. Removing explicit rule features or action masking lowers final quality; the other two ablations do not establish independent gains after correction. These results give a concrete baseline for testing the learned policy under less regular transition patterns.

Deletion and augmentation contribute different candidates on the same documents. At equal call budgets, augmentation alone produces more constraint candidates. Direct execution also reveals a substantial gap between archived candidate vocabulary and the designed rule suite. The next step is to validate those mappings and connect generated rules to the policy environment, then evaluate repairs on natural defects and held-out domains. This would test whether text-derived rule acquisition changes the value of adaptive scheduling.
