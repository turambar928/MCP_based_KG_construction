\begin{abstract}
Knowledge-graph quality control couples two decisions that are usually treated separately: which constraints to acquire and which detected graph defects to repair under a limited budget. We formulate this interaction as a finite-horizon Markov decision process and learn a Double-DQN meta-policy over four executable graph operations and four rule-management operations. A complementary dual-strategy LLM module expands the candidate rule space through deletion completion and augmentation expansion. In an executable controlled-corruption benchmark with actual graph mutations, ten independently trained policies achieve normalized final joint quality of $0.9812\pm0.0013$. Relative to the strongest non-oracle fixed schedule, Double DQN improves final quality by 0.0065 and trajectory AUC by 0.0166 ($p=0.000977$ for both paired one-sided Wilcoxon tests) while halving rule-generation calls from eight to four. Archived generation logs show that combining the two strategies yields 71.9\% more canonicalized candidates than the stronger single strategy. On the designed RuleTest-94 suite, the two executable rule families cover disjoint defect groups and their union detects all 64 defective cases without flagging the 30 clean cases. Deterministic and API-backed extraction tests further characterize structural repair, while explicit scope controls distinguish controlled-suite coverage from open-world accuracy.
\end{abstract}
