\begin{abstract}
Knowledge-graph quality control couples two decisions: which constraints to acquire and which graph defects to repair under a limited budget. We formulate this interaction as a finite-horizon control problem and train a Double-DQN policy over four graph operations and four rule-management operations. A dual-strategy module proposes text-derived rule candidates through deletion completion and augmentation expansion. On an executable controlled-corruption benchmark, ten trained Double-DQN policies reach mean normalized final quality 0.98122 with four accounted acquisition calls. They improve on feasible fixed schedules, while a simple acquire-then-deficit heuristic reaches 0.98248 at the same cost. Forty retrained ablation models show that explicit rule features and action masking affect final quality; the independent effects of graph features and the acquisition penalty are not significant after multiplicity correction. Replaying 9,456 archived generation responses shows complementary candidates on the same documents, but augmentation alone yields more constraint candidates at equal call budgets. A provenance-based audit compiles 18,143 exact typed patterns, of which two match the designed RuleTest-94 suite; their union detects 10 of 64 defects with no false positives. The results characterize learned scheduling, candidate diversity, and executable rule coverage separately, identifying rule alignment and integration as the next steps toward a complete text-driven co-optimization system.
\end{abstract}
