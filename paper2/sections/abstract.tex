\begin{abstract}
Knowledge-graph quality control couples two decisions: which constraints to acquire and which graph defects to repair under a limited budget. We formulate this interaction as a finite-horizon control problem and train a Double-DQN policy over four graph operations and four rule-management operations. A dual-strategy module proposes text-derived rules through deletion completion and augmentation expansion. An action-level audit reveals that a count-based penalty for introduced violations suppresses relation repair because restoration can create temporary duplicates. We compare count, initial-size rate and zero penalties using forty neural models, ten fitted one-step controls, and separate development and test scenarios. On thirty unseen corruptions of the same base graph, rate-penalty Double DQN improves reference-triple F1 from 94.67\% to 99.49\% and restores 94.00\% of the original invalid relations. A simple acquire-then-deficit heuristic reaches 99.91\% F1; the study establishes reward sensitivity without a learned-policy advantage over the strongest simple controls. Replaying 9,456 archived responses shows complementary rule candidates on the same documents, while augmentation alone yields more constraints at equal call budgets. An execution audit compiles 18,143 exact typed patterns; their union detects 10 of 64 defects in RuleTest-94. A separate offline bridge activates these patterns and removes ten prohibited records while retaining all thirty clean records. The results connect reward design to repair behavior and distinguish candidate diversity, executable coverage and reference-fact restoration.
\end{abstract}
